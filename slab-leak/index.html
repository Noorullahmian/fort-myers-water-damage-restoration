Slab Leak Water Damage in Fort Myers

A warm spot on the tile, running water sounds with everything off, or a water bill that suddenly doubled? Those are classic signs of a slab leak, a water line leaking under your concrete slab, and in Lee County nearly every home sits on one. Slab leak water damage spreads quietly under floors and up walls.

Slab Leak Water Damage Help for Fort Myers and Cape Coral Homes

Most Fort Myers and Cape Coral homes are built on concrete slabs, with water lines running in or under the slab.

A plumber finds and repairs the leak; the wet slab, flooring, baseboards and drywall still need to be measured and dried.

Hot water line leaks are the most common kind people notice, because they warm the floor.

Think you have a slab leak? Do this first

  1. Check the water meter. Turn off every faucet, appliance and the irrigation. If the meter still moves, water is escaping somewhere.
  2. Shut off the main water if the floor is wet or the meter is spinning fast.
  3. Turn off the water heater at its breaker, or set a gas heater to pilot, if you suspect the hot water line.
  4. Photograph the wet areas, warm spots, stained grout and damp baseboards.
  5. Call a plumber for leak detection, then start drying the same day.

Signs of a slab leak

  • A warm or hot spot on tile or vinyl floors, usually from a hot water line
  • The sound of running water when nothing is on
  • A water meter that moves with every fixture off
  • A sudden jump in your water bill
  • Damp or swollen baseboards with no visible leak above
  • Dark or wet grout lines, loose or cracked tile, or vinyl plank that lifts
  • Musty smell or mold along the bottom of walls
  • Low water pressure or a water heater that runs constantly
  • Cracks in the slab or water pooling near the foundation outside

Why slab leaks happen in Southwest Florida homes

Slab-on-grade construction is the norm in Fort Myers, Cape Coral, Lehigh Acres and most of Lee County. Water supply lines run below or inside the concrete, so when one fails, the water has nowhere to go but sideways under your floors. A leak you can see above the floor is a different job; see burst pipe cleanup.

Common causes:

  • Corrosion of older copper lines, which can develop pinholes over time
  • Abrasion where a pipe rubs against concrete, gravel or another pipe as it expands and contracts with hot water
  • Ground movement and settling in sandy soil
  • High water pressure that stresses every joint
  • Poor installation or damaged pipe during original construction

Not every leak under the slab is a supply line. A cracked drain line carries wastewater instead; see cast iron pipe water damage.

How plumbers find and fix slab leaks

Finding the leak comes first. Plumbers typically use pressure tests to confirm which line is leaking, then acoustic listening equipment and thermal imaging to pinpoint where.

Repair options usually include:

  • Spot repair: breaking out a small section of slab, fixing the pipe and patching the concrete
  • Reroute: abandoning the leaking line and running a new one through the walls or attic
  • Repipe: replacing the home's supply lines when leaks keep coming back

Ask the plumber for a written report showing which line failed and how. You'll need it for the insurance claim.

How slab leak water damage is dried

  1. The plumber repairs the line and confirms it holds pressure.
  2. Water is extracted from carpet and the floor surface with emergency water removal equipment.
  3. Moisture is mapped across the slab and up the walls with meters and a thermal camera. The wet area is often much bigger than the warm spot.
  4. Flooring that traps water is lifted, baseboards are pulled and drywall is opened where needed.
  5. The slab and walls are dried with dehumidifiers and air movers, and sometimes specialty equipment for concrete, with readings logged daily. See structural drying.
  6. Repairs follow once the slab and walls read dry: flooring, baseboards, drywall and cabinets.

Concrete dries more slowly than drywall, so slab leak drying often takes longer than the 3 to 5 days typical of a surface leak.

Is slab leak water clean?

A leaking supply line carries clean water, Category 1, when it's caught early. But water that has sat under flooring for days or weeks can degrade to Category 2, and long-standing moisture under vinyl plank or carpet often brings mold. The longer a slab leak runs, the more material comes out.

Does insurance cover a slab leak in Florida?

Coverage varies a lot by policy:

  • The resulting water damage to floors, walls and cabinets is often covered when the leak was sudden.
  • The pipe repair itself may not be covered.
  • Tear-out coverage, in some policies, pays to break out the slab or open walls to reach the leak.
  • Long-running leaks may be excluded as continuous or repeated seepage.

Keep the plumber's leak detection report, photos of the damage and your water bills, which can show when the leak started. Read how insurance handles water damage in Florida.

How to lower the risk of another slab leak

  • Have your water pressure checked and install or adjust a pressure-reducing valve if it's high.
  • Watch your water bill and check the meter if it jumps.
  • Install a leak-detecting shut-off valve that closes the main when it senses continuous flow.
  • Ask about rerouting or repiping if you've had more than one slab leak.

Questions about slab leak water damage

How do I know if I have a slab leak?

Turn off every fixture and check the water meter. If it moves, look for warm spots on the floor, damp baseboards or the sound of running water, and call a plumber for leak detection.

Is a slab leak an emergency?

Yes, if water is surfacing on the floor or the meter is spinning fast. Shut off the main water and start drying. A slow leak still needs prompt attention before it damages flooring and grows mold.

Can tile be saved after a slab leak?

Often, if it's still firmly bonded. The grout lines and slab are dried in place. Loose, hollow or cracked tile is replaced.

How long does it take to dry a slab after a leak?

Longer than a surface leak. Concrete releases water slowly, so slab drying often takes more than the 3 to 5 days typical for drywall and carpet.

Should I reroute or repair the leaking line?

That's the plumber's call. One leak in an otherwise sound line is often spot-repaired; repeat leaks or old copper often lead to rerouting or repiping.

Can a slab leak cause mold?

Yes. Moisture trapped under vinyl plank, carpet or baseboards for days or weeks can grow mold, especially in Florida humidity. Drying should start as soon as the line is repaired.

Can I stay in my house while the slab is dried?

Usually yes for a clean-water leak, with equipment running in the affected rooms. Large tear-outs or kitchen damage may make some rooms unusable for a while.

Shut off the water, call a plumber for leak detection, and get the slab and walls dried before the damage spreads.
