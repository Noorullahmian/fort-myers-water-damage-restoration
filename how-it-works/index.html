Water on the floor and no idea what happens next? Most people have never dealt with water damage before, and the not-knowing is half the stress. Here's how our Fort Myers water damage service works, from the first call to the final dry reading. No surprises, no pressure.

  • We answer 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Step 1: You call

When you call 239-356-3708, you'll be asked a few quick questions:

  • Your address and ZIP code
  • What happened and when you found it
  • Where the water came from: a supply line, appliance, AC, toilet, drain, roof or flooding
  • Which rooms are wet, and roughly how big the wet area is
  • Whether the water and power are off
  • Whether you own or rent, and whether it's a condo
  • Your insurance company, if you plan to file a claim

The answers help decide what equipment is needed and what to do while you wait. If the water is still running, you'll be walked through shutting it off.

Step 2: While you wait

Before anyone arrives, you can protect your home and your claim:

  • Shut off the water at the fixture or the main.
  • Cut power at the breaker to wet rooms.
  • Photograph and video everything before you move or throw anything away.
  • Move belongings off wet floors.
  • Keep the AC running to help pull moisture out of the air.

The full list is on the first-hour water damage checklist.

Step 3: The inspection

When the technician arrives, they should:

  • Confirm the source is stopped, or tell you who needs to fix it, such as a plumber, roofer or HVAC technician.
  • Identify the water category: clean water (Category 1), gray water (Category 2) or contaminated water such as sewage or floodwater (Category 3). This decides what can be dried and what has to be removed.
  • Measure moisture in floors, walls, cabinets and ceilings with moisture meters and often a thermal camera.
  • Map the wet area, which is usually bigger than what you can see.
  • Explain what they found in plain language and show you the readings.

Step 4: A written estimate

You should get a written estimate before work beyond emergency water extraction begins. It should explain:

  • What will be removed and why
  • What equipment will be used and for about how long
  • The expected cost, based on the square footage, water category, materials and drying days
  • What isn't included, such as plumbing repairs or rebuilding

If anything changes during drying, such as finding more water inside a wall, the estimate should be updated in writing and explained. See typical ranges in the water damage cost guide.

Step 5: Water removal

Standing water is pulled out with truck-mounted or portable extractors. Extraction removes far more water than drying ever could, which shortens drying time and limits damage. See emergency water removal.

Step 6: Removing what can't be saved

Some materials can't be dried in place and are removed:

  • Carpet pad soaked with water
  • Baseboards that have swelled
  • The lower part of drywall when it's soaked, contaminated or hiding water
  • Wet insulation inside walls or ceilings
  • Particle board cabinet bases and shelves that have swelled
  • Vinyl plank or laminate that's trapping water against the slab

Everything removed should be photographed and listed for your insurer.

Step 7: Drying

Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers are set up to dry the structure. Each day, moisture readings are taken and logged, and equipment is moved or adjusted as areas dry. Most clean-water jobs take about 3 to 5 days. Floodwater, sewage, concrete slabs and leaks that sat for a while can take longer. See how structural drying works.

During drying:

  • Equipment runs around the clock and makes noise.
  • Keep doors and windows closed unless told otherwise.
  • Keep the AC running.
  • Don't turn equipment off without asking.

Step 8: Final readings and paperwork

When readings show the materials are back to normal, drying is done. You should receive:

  • Photos from before, during and after
  • The moisture map and daily drying logs
  • A list of materials removed
  • A final invoice that matches the written estimate, or explains any changes

These records are what your insurance adjuster will want to see.

Step 9: Repairs

After drying, the drywall, baseboards, flooring and cabinets are rebuilt. Rebuilding is your decision, and you choose who does it.

What you won't be asked to do

  • Sign over your insurance benefits. For Florida residential policies issued on or after January 1, 2023, post-loss assignment of benefits isn't allowed.
  • Pay for work you didn't agree to in writing.
  • Make a decision on the spot about rebuilding.

Insurance: how the paperwork fits

  • You file the claim with your insurer and deal with your adjuster.
  • Photos, moisture readings and drying logs from the job support your claim.
  • Your insurer may send an adjuster to inspect the damage.
  • You're responsible for your deductible and anything not covered.
  • Florida rules give you deadlines to report a claim.

What the water category means for your job

CategoryExamplesWhat usually happens
Category 1: clean waterBurst supply line, water heater, AC condensate caught earlyMost materials can be dried in place if drying starts quickly
Category 2: gray waterWasher or dishwasher drain water, toilet overflow without fecesCarpet pad and porous materials often removed; surfaces cleaned and treated
Category 3: contaminated waterSewage, floodwater, storm surgePorous materials removed; surfaces disinfected; protective gear used

Water can get worse over time. A clean leak left for days can become Category 2, which is why starting the same day matters.

What the drying equipment does

  • Air movers blow air across wet surfaces so moisture evaporates faster.
  • Dehumidifiers pull that moisture out of the air so it doesn't soak back into walls and floors.
  • Specialty equipment can push dry air into wall cavities, under cabinets and across concrete slabs.
  • Moisture meters and thermal cameras track progress, so drying ends when the readings say it's dry, not when it looks dry.
