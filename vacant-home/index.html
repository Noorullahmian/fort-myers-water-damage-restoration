Found Water Damage in Your Fort Myers Home After Time Away? Do This First

  • Shut off the main water if you find active water or don't know the source. The valve is usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  • Check for safety. If water is near outlets or the breaker panel, or the ceiling sags, stay out of that area and cut power at the breaker if it's safe to reach.
  • Photograph and video everything before touching it: wet floors, stains, mold, the water heater, under sinks, the AC closet and the thermostat reading.
  • Write down the date and time you found it, and when you last were in the house or when it was last checked.
  • Open nothing up yet. Don't pull baseboards or tear out drywall before photos, because the adjuster may want to see it as found.
  • Call your insurer to report the loss, and get drying started. Waiting makes the damage, and the claim, worse.

What usually fails while a Fort Myers home sits empty

Southwest Florida's summer heat, humidity and storms are hard on a closed-up house. These are the failures found most often when owners come back:

  • AC failure or power loss. If the AC stops, from a breakdown or a storm power outage, indoor humidity climbs and mold can start growing on walls, furniture and closets within days.
  • AC condensate overflow. The AC keeps running but the drain line clogs, and water spills into the closet, ceiling or floor for weeks. See AC leak water damage.
  • Water heater leaks. Tanks fail at the bottom and leak slowly, or burst all at once in a garage or closet.
  • Supply line failures. Braided lines to toilets, sinks, washers and ice makers fail without warning. See burst pipe cleanup.
  • Roof and window leaks from summer downpours and tropical storms, often showing up as ceiling stains.
  • Chewed lines. Rodents in quiet houses sometimes chew through plastic dishwasher, ice maker or washer hoses.
  • Leaks from the unit above in condos, where your neighbor may also be away. See condo water damage.

That musty smell: humidity or water damage?

A closed-up house in Southwest Florida can smell musty from humidity alone. But the smell can also come from a hidden leak or mold behind walls.

Check indoor humidity with an inexpensive hygrometer. Indoor relative humidity is generally best kept below 60 percent; readings well above that mean the AC wasn't keeping up.

Look for visible mold on walls, ceilings, closet contents and the AC return grille.

Feel the bottoms of walls and baseboards near bathrooms, the kitchen and the AC closet for dampness or softness.

Watch the water meter. With every fixture off, a moving meter means a leak somewhere.

If you see mold on the air handler or vents, have the AC checked before running it hard, so it doesn't spread spores through the ducts.

How long-running leaks change the cleanup

A leak that ran for weeks is a different job from one caught the same day.

Water category drifts. Clean water left sitting for 24 to 48 hours or more can become Category 2, and long-standing water often needs to be treated as more contaminated.

More material comes out. Drywall, baseboards, cabinet bases and vinyl plank that stayed wet for weeks usually can't be saved.

Slabs hold more water. Concrete under tile and plank soaks up water and takes longer to dry.

Mold is more likely. If mold has grown, bring in a qualified Florida mold remediator to remove it.

How water damage in a vacant Fort Myers home is dried

Find the source and stop it, with a plumber, HVAC technician or roofer as needed.

Map the moisture. Meters and a thermal camera show how far water spread under floors and into walls, which is often far beyond the visible stain.

Extract standing water and remove soaked carpet pad with emergency water removal.

Remove what can't be saved, photographing each item and material for the claim.

Dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers, logging readings daily. See how structural drying works.

Bring humidity down in the whole house so closets, furniture and the rest of the structure stop absorbing moisture.

Insurance when nobody was home

Water claims on seasonal homes raise a few extra questions:

Vacancy and unoccupancy clauses. Many policies limit coverage for certain losses if a home is vacant or unoccupied for a set number of days. Read yours, or ask your agent, before you file.

Sudden vs gradual. A supply line that burst is treated differently from a slow leak that ran for months. Report the date you found it honestly.

Proof of care. Home watch reports, utility bills showing normal water use, and smart-thermostat or leak-sensor logs help show the home was checked and when the damage began.

Mold limits. Many Florida policies cap mold coverage.

Tell it plainly. Tell the adjuster when you left, how the house was checked while you were away, and when you found the damage. Don't guess at the cause; let the inspection show it.

How to protect the house next time you leave

Shut off the main water before you go, and turn off the water heater at its breaker or set it to vacation.

Leave the AC running and set it to control humidity, rather than turning it off. Ask your HVAC company for the right setting for your system.

Have the AC drain line flushed before you leave.

Arrange home watch visits on a schedule, with written reports and photos, and a visit after every major storm.

Install leak sensors or an automatic water shut-off valve that alert your phone.

Replace old braided supply lines and washer hoses before they fail.

Questions about water damage in vacant homes

I came back to a wet floor. Can I just dry it with fans?

Only if it's a small, recent spill on a hard floor. If water sat for days or reached under flooring, cabinets or walls, it needs moisture mapping and commercial drying, or it will keep feeding mold.

Will insurance cover water damage if my house was empty?

It depends on your policy's vacancy or unoccupancy wording and on whether the leak was sudden or gradual. Many claims are paid; some are limited. Read the policy and document everything before cleanup.

The AC was off when I got back and there's mold. What now?

Don't run the AC hard until it's checked, keep windows closed, and photograph the mold. For removal, bring in a qualified Florida mold remediator. Drying and humidity control stop it from getting worse.

How long does drying take after a long leak?

Clean-water drying often takes about 3 to 5 days, but long-running leaks usually need tear-out first and more time on concrete slabs.

Can home watch prevent this?

It can't stop a pipe from failing, but regular visits catch leaks early, and documented visits help with insurance questions.

What's the first thing to check when I get back?

Smell the air, then check the AC closet, under the kitchen sink, around toilets and the water heater. Those are where most leaks in empty homes start.
