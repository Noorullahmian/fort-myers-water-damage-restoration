AC Leaking Through the Ceiling in Your Fort Myers Home? Do This First

  • Turn the AC off at the thermostat. This stops new condensate from forming. If water is near the air handler's electrical parts, switch off its breaker too.
  • Cut power to wet fixtures. If water drips from a light, fan or smoke detector, turn off that circuit at the breaker and keep clear.
  • Catch the water. Put a bucket and towels under the drip and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way.
  • Check the ceiling. If it sags or bulges, keep everyone out from underneath it. A water-filled ceiling can come down without warning. Read what to do about a leaking ceiling.
  • Look at the air handler. In a closet or garage unit, check whether the drain pan is full. In an attic unit, don't climb up if the ceiling below is wet and soft.
  • Photograph everything before cleanup: the stain, the drip, the pan, the wet floor.

Why AC units leak water inside Fort Myers homes

Your air conditioner removes moisture from the air as it cools. That water collects on the evaporator coil, drips into a drain pan and leaves the house through a small PVC condensate line. In humid Southwest Florida, that system handles a lot of water: one Florida HVAC company estimates 20 to 30 gallons a day for a typical home system. When any part of the path fails, the water goes into your house instead.

The most common causes:

  • Clogged condensate drain line. Algae, slime and dust build up inside the line. It is the single most common reason AC units leak indoors in Florida.
  • Full or cracked drain pan. Older metal pans rust through; plastic pans crack. Water then leaks before it reaches the drain.
  • Failed float switch. This safety switch is meant to shut the AC off when the pan fills. If it is missing, stuck or wired wrong, the pan overflows.
  • Frozen evaporator coil. A dirty filter or low refrigerant can freeze the coil. When it thaws, more water comes off than the pan can handle.
  • Disconnected or sagging drain line. A joint pulls apart in the attic or wall and drips straight onto drywall.
  • Condensate pump failure. Units that pump water out (common in condos and interior closets) leak when the pump stops.
  • Sweating ducts and vents. Cold supply ducts in a hot attic collect condensation that drips through ceiling vents.

Signs of AC water damage you may not have noticed

AC leaks are often slow. They can run for days before anyone sees water, especially with attic units.

  • Brown or yellow stains on the ceiling near the air handler or a vent
  • Bubbling paint or soft drywall in the hallway next to the AC closet
  • Water stains or swelling on baseboards beside the air handler
  • Carpet that feels damp near the closet, or vinyl plank that lifts at the edges
  • A musty smell when the AC runs
  • Rust on the bottom of the air handler cabinet or a pan that is always wet
  • The AC shutting off on its own, which can mean the float switch tripped

AC repair vs water damage restoration: who fixes what?

An HVAC technician fixes the cause: clears the drain line, replaces the pan, resets or installs the float switch, or repairs a frozen coil. That stops new water.

They don't dry your house. Water already in the ceiling, walls, insulation and floor stays there unless it is measured and dried. In Florida humidity, wet drywall and insulation rarely dry on their own fast enough to prevent mold and swelling.

Water damage restoration covers the rest:

  • Moisture mapping with meters and a thermal camera to find every wet area, including inside walls and above the ceiling.
  • Removing what can't be dried, such as soaked insulation, collapsed or sagging drywall and swollen baseboards.
  • Drying with dehumidifiers and air movers, with readings logged daily. See how structural drying works.
  • Documentation for your insurance claim: photos, moisture logs and a list of what was removed.

How AC leak damage is dried and repaired in Fort Myers homes

Ceiling. A small stain from a short leak can often be dried in place. A sagging, soft or stained-through ceiling is usually cut out and replaced, and wet insulation above it is removed.

Walls. Drywall next to an air handler closet often wicks water up from the floor. It can usually be dried in place if caught early; if it stayed wet for days, the bottom of the wall may be cut out.

Floors. Tile on slab usually stays and is dried. Luxury vinyl plank often has to be lifted because it traps water on the concrete. Carpet pad near the closet is usually replaced.

Closet and cabinetry. The air handler platform, closet baseboards and any particle board nearby are checked for swelling.

Most clean-water AC leaks dry in about 3 to 5 days once drying starts. Leaks that ran for weeks take longer and usually mean more tear-out.

Is AC condensate water clean?

AC condensate is usually treated as Category 1, or clean, water when it is caught quickly. But the drain pan and line can hold algae and bacteria, and any water that sits for 24 to 48 hours can degrade to Category 2. If the leak has run for days, plan on antimicrobial treatment and more material removal.

Does homeowners insurance cover AC leak water damage in Florida?

It depends on how the leak happened. Florida policies usually separate sudden damage from slow, long-term leaks.

  • Often covered: water damage from a sudden overflow, such as a drain line that clogs and spills, a pan that cracks or a coil that thaws all at once.
  • Often excluded: damage from a leak that ran for weeks, which insurers may call gradual seepage or lack of maintenance.
  • Usually not covered: the cost to repair the AC unit itself. The policy typically pays for the resulting water damage, not the equipment.
  • Mold coverage is often capped.

Report the date you found the leak truthfully, photograph everything and keep the HVAC technician's invoice, since it shows the cause. Read how insurance handles water damage in Florida.

How to stop AC leaks from happening again

  • Have the condensate line flushed and checked at least once a year; many Florida homeowners do it twice because algae grows fast here.
  • Replace the air filter on schedule. A clogged filter can freeze the coil.
  • Make sure a working float switch is installed and tested.
  • Check that attic units sit over a secondary drain pan and that the pan is clean and dry.
  • Look at the air handler closet once a month for water in the pan or rust on the cabinet.
  • If you leave for the season, have someone check the AC regularly. See water damage in vacant homes.

Questions about AC leak water damage

Why is my AC leaking water inside the house?

Most often because the condensate drain line is clogged, so water backs up and overflows the drain pan. Other causes include a cracked pan, a failed float switch, a frozen coil or a disconnected drain line.

Can I keep running my AC if it's leaking?

No. Turn it off until the cause is fixed. Running it keeps producing water that ends up in your ceiling or walls.

Is a small ceiling stain from the AC an emergency?

Not always, but it means water has already soaked through insulation and drywall. Have it checked within a day or two, because hidden moisture in Florida humidity can lead to mold within 24 to 48 hours.

Will the HVAC company fix the ceiling?

Usually not. HVAC companies fix the air conditioner. Drying and repairing the ceiling, walls and floor is water damage restoration work.

How long does it take to dry after an AC leak?

Most clean-water AC leaks dry in about 3 to 5 days with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers. Longer leaks and wet slabs take more time.

Can I just paint over an AC water stain?

Only after the area is confirmed dry with a moisture meter and the leak is fixed. Painting over wet drywall traps moisture and the stain usually comes back.
