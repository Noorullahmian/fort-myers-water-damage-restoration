Condo leaks in Estero: who pays?

Florida law, Statute 718.111(11), generally splits condo water damage between the association and owners based on what's damaged and what the condo documents say. As a general pattern:

  • The association's master policy usually covers the building structure and common elements.
  • Your HO-6 policy usually covers your interior finishes, improvements and belongings.
  • The cause and your condo documents decide the details, and negligence can shift responsibility.

Read your declaration of condominium and talk to your insurer. See condo water damage for more.

Returning to Estero for the season

If you're coming back after months away:

  • Check the AC first. A failed AC in summer means high humidity throughout the house.
  • Look and smell for musty odors, stains on ceilings and walls, and damp baseboards.
  • Check under sinks, around the water heater and behind the washer.
  • Photograph anything you find and note the date you discovered it.

How water damage restoration works in Estero homes

  1. Stop the source. A plumber, HVAC technician or building maintenance fixes the cause.
  2. Extract standing water with emergency water removal equipment.
  3. Map the moisture in your unit and, in condos, neighboring units if needed.
  4. Remove what can't be dried, such as soaked carpet pad, swollen baseboards and wet drywall.
  5. Dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers, logging readings daily. See how structural drying works.
  6. Repair once everything reads dry, coordinated with the association where needed.

Seasonal homes in Estero: preventing summer damage

Summer is when Estero homes are most at risk, because the heat and humidity peak while many owners are away. A few steps make a big difference:

  • Shut off the main water and the water heater before you leave.
  • Set the AC to control humidity rather than turning it off.
  • Have the AC drain line cleared before summer.
  • Arrange regular home checks by a home watch service, neighbor or property manager.
  • Install leak alarms or a smart shut-off valve that alerts you by phone.
  • Leave interior doors open so air moves through the house.

Coach homes, villas and shared walls

Coach homes and villas often share a roof, walls or floors with a neighbor. A leak in one home can show up in the other first. If water appears on a shared wall or ceiling, contact your neighbor and the association quickly, shut off your own water if the source is unclear, and photograph both sides if you can.

AC closet overflows

Many Estero condos and villas have the air handler in a closet off a hallway. When the condensate line clogs, the drain pan overflows and soaks the carpet, baseboards and the bottom of the closet walls, and in upper floors it can reach the unit below. Have the line flushed regularly and make sure the float switch works.

Who pays for condo water damage in Estero?

It depends on Florida Statute 718.111(11), your condo documents and the cause. Generally, the association covers the structure and you cover your interior finishes and belongings.

I came back for the season and the house smells musty. Is that water damage?

It could be high humidity from an AC problem or a hidden leak. Check the AC, look for stains and damp areas, and have the home checked with a moisture meter.

How do I find hidden water after a condo leak?
